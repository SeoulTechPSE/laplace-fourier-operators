Laplace-type neural operators represent the transient part of a solution by learned poles and residues, and these poles are often read as the system's natural frequencies and decay rates. We test that reading. On linear systems with known poles we audit LNO, ALNO and LFNO in 900 training runs, as single pole–residue layers and as full architectures. Under the published protocols the poles cannot move far enough, because the learning-rate schedule caps their travel. Once they can move, the lightly damped complex poles are found. Fast real poles are not found by any published recipe, and their profiles are flat within the layer's own floor. Models fit equally well with right or wrong poles, so test error does not certify poles. On nonlinear systems the comparable quantity is the small-signal pole, the pole of the linearization at the operating point. We build a rule that reads a stored pole as physical only when two probes of the trained operator and a check on the training data agree. It took four versions, each pre-registered before the test that followed, over five systems, 18 data sets and 375 trained models. The fourth version makes no false trust in any of them. On a system it had not seen it trusts 8 of 75 models, all within 4 %; on 360 later models it trusts 167 with 2 false trusts, both in a few-pole variant, and a fifth check removes the larger one at no cost. The published LNO configuration never moves its poles, and its small-signal Jacobian is non-causal because of a channel-index quirk in the original code; with the textbook wiring the rule certifies its stored poles in 37 of 90 runs, with no false trust.
