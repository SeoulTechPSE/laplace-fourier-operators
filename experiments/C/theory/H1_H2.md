# H1, H2 and H3 for a single pole-residue layer: statements and proofs (draft)

Project (C), plan `docs/proposal_C_identifiability.qmd`, Section 4. Draft of 2026-11-17.
Numerical checks: `h12_check.py` → `results/h12_check.md` (H1, H2); `h3_check.py` → `results/h3_check.md` (H3).

The plan states three hypotheses. This note restates H1, H2 and H3 as precise statements for
one single-channel layer and proves them (H3: Section 3b).

**Scope and assumptions.**

- One single-channel pole-residue layer on the DFT grid, with real input and real output.
- The data are noise-free and generated by the model class itself.
- Noise is the subject of E4: the Fisher information below is the inverse of the Cramér–Rao
  bound used there.
- Model mismatch, such as the data's input hold, is discussed in the remarks after Lemma 1.

**Main results.**

| Plan | This note | Result |
|---|---|---|
| H2, tied layer | Theorem 2, Proposition 3 | **Holds, in a sharper form.** One input with sparse DFT support identifies every pole that the input excites in the transient. Persistent excitation of order M + M′ identifies all poles even without that. Excitation is sufficient, not necessary. For a generic single input the layer is locally identifiable. |
| H1, free steady branch | Theorem 4, Proposition 5 | **Holds exactly when the input set has rank one per DFT line** (for example one input). Then any poles and residues reproduce the data. With two or more generic inputs the poles are locally identifiable, but the information is reduced by a per-line projection. The plan's "for any ε" form is not true in general. |
| H3, partial tie | Theorem 6 | **Holds as a Lipschitz bound on the deviation from the tied estimate**, not from the truth. With exact data the minimiser is the truth for every δ. With a model mismatch the poles move by −H⁻¹G R̂_δ, which is at most C δ. The constant C is computable without the truth. The deviation saturates at the δ where the bound on R_res stops being active. |

## 1. Setting

**Grid and DFT.**

- Samples are t_k = kΔ, k = 0, …, n − 1, over the window T = nΔ.
- The DFT lines are ω_l = 2πl/T for l ∈ {−⌈n/2⌉ + 1, …, ⌊n/2⌋}, with nodes z_l = e^{iω_l Δ}.
- An input is x ∈ ℝⁿ, with DFT α_l(x) = Σ_k x_k e^{−iω_l t_k} and support S(x) = {l : α_l ≠ 0}.

**Parameters.** θ = {(μ_m, β_m)}, m = 1, …, M, is a set closed under conjugation:
(μ, β) ∈ θ implies (μ̄, β̄) ∈ θ. M counts conjugates, so it is 4 for p4 and 6 for p6.

The E1 layer's slots are not conjugate-symmetric, but the real part taken in its output makes
each slot (μ, β) act as the pair {(μ, β/2), (μ̄, β̄/2)}. A slot on the real axis acts as
(μ, Re β). (The proof is a re-indexing l → −l, using α_{−l} = ᾱ_l.)

**Assumptions (A).**

- (A1) Stable poles: Re μ_m < 0.
- (A2) No aliasing: |Im μ_m| < π/Δ.
- (A3) Distinct poles.
- (A4) β_m ≠ 0.

Under (A2) and (A3) the pole nodes w_m = e^{μ_m Δ} are distinct. Under (A1) they lie inside the
unit disk, while the input nodes z_l lie on the unit circle.

**Tied layer** (LNO v1; the arithmetic of `identifiability.layer_out` and `PoleResidueTS1d`):

  (A_θ x)_k = (1/n) Σ_l α_l [ K_θ(iω_l) z_l^k − Σ_m β_m e^{μ_m t_k} / (iω_l − μ_m) ],
  K_θ(s) = Σ_m β_m / (s − μ_m).

It splits as A_θ = C_θ + T_θ.

- C_θ is circulant, with symbol K_θ(iω_l). It is the steady branch.
- T_θ is the transient branch: T_θ x = −Σ_m β_m s_m(x) e^{μ_m t}, where
  s_m(x) = (1/n) Σ_l α_l / (iω_l − μ_m).

s_m(x) is the value at t = 0 of the periodic steady-state response of the mode 1/(s − μ_m) to
x. Mode m is *excited in the transient* by x if s_m(x) ≠ 0.

### Lemma 1 (what the tied layer computes)

(A_θ x)_k = y(t_k), where y is the zero-initial-state response of K_θ to the trigonometric
interpolant x̃(t) = (1/n) Σ_l α_l e^{iω_l t}. (For even n, take the real part of the Nyquist
term.)

*Proof.* For the input e^{iωt}, the zero-initial-state response of 1/(s − μ) is
(e^{iωt} − e^{μt}) / (iω − μ). Sum over l and m with weights α_l/n and β_m. ∎

*Check:* against an independent first-order-hold solve of x̃ on a 32× finer grid, the relative
difference is 1.6e-5 (p4) and 4.9e-6 (p6). That is the hold's O(h²) error.

*Remarks.*

1. **The layer assumes a band-limited input hold.** Data generated with another hold, such as
   the linear interpolation of `lfo.data.lti.response` used for E1, are not exactly in the
   model class. This is the representation floor of E1 and E2 (5e-4 to 2e-3). It hides p6's
   −1 / −2 (E2 phase 1), and removing it is why the first-order-hold refit of E4 (fourth pass)
   identifies those poles.
2. **The transient branch has a fixed role.** It cancels the periodic steady state at t = 0,
   mode by mode. It is a rank-M operator whose range is spanned by {e^{μ_m t}}.

### Lemma 2 (exponential sums)

If w_1, …, w_P ∈ ℂ∖{0} are distinct, P ≤ n, and Σ_p c_p w_p^k = 0 for k = 0, …, n − 1, then
c = 0.

*Proof.* The n × P Vandermonde matrix [w_p^k] has full column rank. ∎

## 2. H2: the tied layer

### Theorem 2 (global uniqueness from one sparse input)

Let θ and θ′ satisfy (A), with M and M′ terms. Let x be any input in the span of the training
inputs, with support S = S(x), and suppose |S| + M + M′ ≤ n. If A_θ x = A_θ′ x, then:

- **(i) The steady values agree on the support:** K_θ(iω_l) = K_θ′(iω_l) for every l ∈ S.
- **(ii) The excited poles are identified:** the sets
  {(μ_m, β_m s_m(x)) : β_m s_m(x) ≠ 0} for θ and θ′ are equal. In particular, every pole of
  the truth that x excites in the transient is a pole of θ′, with the same residue.
- **(iii) With persistent excitation of order M + M′:** if |S| ≥ M + M′, then θ = θ′ as sets,
  whether or not the poles are excited in the transient.

*Proof.*

- **Write the outputs as exponential sums.** (A_θ x)_k is an exponential sum in k with nodes
  {z_l : l ∈ S}, coefficients α_l K_θ(iω_l)/n, and nodes {w_m} with coefficients
  −β_m s_m(x). The same holds for θ′.
- **Count the nodes of the difference.** The z_l are distinct points on the unit circle. The
  pole nodes lie strictly inside the disk, are distinct within each set, and coincide between
  θ and θ′ only for equal poles. So the difference A_θ x − A_θ′ x is an exponential sum with
  at most |S| + M + M′ ≤ n distinct nodes.
- **Apply Lemma 2.** Every coefficient of the difference vanishes.
  - At z_l this gives α_l (K_θ − K_θ′)(iω_l) = 0, which is (i), since α_l ≠ 0 on S.
  - At a pole node w it gives β_m s_m − β′_{m′} s′_{m′} = 0 for a pole shared by both sets,
    and β_m s_m = 0 for a pole in one set only. This is (ii).
- **Persistent excitation gives (iii).** Over a common denominator,
  K_θ − K_θ′ = P/Q with deg P ≤ M + M′ − 1. By (i), P vanishes at the |S| ≥ M + M′ distinct
  points iω_l, so P ≡ 0 and K_θ ≡ K_θ′. Partial fractions are unique, and by (A3) and (A4)
  this gives θ = θ′. ∎

*Check:* `h12_check.py` recovers the poles by a matrix pencil of order |S| + M from one tied-
layer output, discarding the unit-circle nodes.

| system | input lines | max relative pole error |
|---|---|---|
| p4 | {3, 11} | 1.4e-13 |
| p6 | {3, 11} | 3.5e-12 |
| p6 | {7}, one sinusoid | 4.9e-13 |

*Remarks.*

1. **(iii) is the plan's H2.** With M = M′ = 2N (N pairs counted twice), persistent
   excitation means |S| ≥ 2N distinct DFT lines. For a real multisine with K sinusoids on
   grid lines, |S| = 2K, so the condition is K ≥ N. With the E1 layer's spare slots,
   M′ = 2 × (number of slots).
2. **(ii) shows that persistent excitation is not necessary for the tied layer.** The
   transient branch carries every excited pole, so one sinusoid identifies all poles exactly
   (p6, one line). This is consistent with E2 phase 1: one sinusoid makes p4 locally
   identifiable (condition number 4e3), and richness changes the conditioning rather than
   identifiability.
3. **Spare slots.** For an over-sized model (M′ > M), (ii) and (iii) say that every spare slot
   has β′ s′ = 0, i.e. it is dead, or coincides with a true pole (merged; (A3) is then
   applied to the merged set). The good-fit, wrong-pole solutions of E1 and E2 are therefore
   not exact fits. They are within the representation floor (Remark 1 after Lemma 1) or at a
   local minimum.
4. **The count |S| + M + M′ ≤ n excludes full-support inputs.** Steps, PRBS and off-grid
   sinusoids (the E2 multisines are log-spaced, off the grid) have |S| = n. Proposition 3
   covers them locally.
5. **(A2) is needed.** Poles with |Im μ| ≥ π/Δ alias onto the same node w. That is the
   aliasing fold of E4 (sixteenth pass).

### Proposition 3 (generic local identifiability from one input)

Let θ* satisfy (A) and use the minimal real parameterisation v: (Re μ, Im μ, Re β, Im β) per
conjugate pair and (μ, β) per real pole, p parameters in all. If n ≥ 2M + 2, then for every
x ∈ ℝⁿ outside a proper algebraic subset (Lebesgue-null), the Jacobian ∂(A_v x)/∂v at v* has
full column rank p.

*Proof.*

- **Reduce to one input.** The Jacobian is linear in x, so its p × p minors are polynomials in
  x. It suffices to exhibit one x with full rank.
- **Choose x = cos(ω_l t)** with 0 < l < n/2. Then s_m(x) = −μ_m / (μ_m² + ω_l²), which is
  nonzero by (A1).
- **Expand the derivatives.**
  - The derivative with respect to β_m is a steady term (in span{e^{±iω_l t}}) plus
    s_m e^{μ_m t}, and its conjugate.
  - The derivative with respect to μ_m is a steady term, plus β_m s_m t e^{μ_m t}, plus a
    multiple of e^{μ_m t}, and its conjugate.
- **Use linear independence.** The 2 + 2M functions e^{±iω_l t}, e^{μ_m t} and t e^{μ_m t}
  are linearly independent on n ≥ 2M + 2 samples (confluent Vandermonde with distinct
  nodes). In these coordinates the Jacobian is block-triangular:
  - only ∂μ_m reaches t e^{μ_m t}, with coefficient β_m s_m ≠ 0;
  - ∂β_m reaches e^{μ_m t} with coefficient s_m ≠ 0.
- **Count real parameters.** For a pair, (d Re μ, d Im μ) ↦ β s (d Re μ + i d Im μ) is a
  real-linear bijection ℝ² → ℂ, and likewise for the residue. So the rank is p. ∎

*Check* (columns scaled to unit norm; full rank in every case):

| system | p | smallest singular value | condition number |
|---|---|---|---|
| p4 | 8 | 1.6e-2 … 8.6e-2 | 20 … 130 |
| p6 | 12 | 4.7e-4 … 2.1e-3 | 990 … 5300 |

The inputs are a grid cosine, two E1 inputs and white noise.

*Remark.* "Generic" is not "every". Proposition 3 gives local, not global, identifiability for
full-support inputs, and says nothing about conditioning. What E1 and E2 measure as practical
identifiability is this Jacobian's conditioning compared with the representation floor, or
with the noise (E4, the CRB). For p6's −1 / −2 on E1 data the Jacobian has full rank, but the
profiled error of a 10 % change is below the band-limited floor (E2 phase 1). Hence they are
identified only after the floor is removed (first-order hold, E4).

## 3. H1: a free steady branch

**Model U** (the ALNO form of E3):

  y = T_θ x + C_R x,

with T_θ the tied transient branch and C_R circulant with a free symbol R_l on a set of lines
L_R, with R_{−l} = R̄_l. The truth is θ*, R* = K_θ*(iω_l). LFNO's R K_θ is the same model
with R K in place of R.

### Theorem 4 (exact non-identifiability with a per-line rank-one input set)

Let L_R be all lines, and let the training inputs x^(1), …, x^(J) satisfy
dim span_j{α_l(x^(j))} ≤ 1 for every l (for example J = 1). Suppose every line l carries some
α_l(x^(j)) ≠ 0. Then for every θ′ (any poles, any residues, any M′) there is an R′ such that
model U with (θ′, R′) reproduces all training outputs exactly.

More generally, for any input set, (θ′, R′) reproduces the outputs if and only if, for every
line l, the vector (D̂_l^(j))_j lies in span (α_l(x^(j)))_j, where D^(j) = (T_θ* − T_θ′) x^(j)
and D̂ is its DFT. On lines outside L_R, or with α_l = 0 for all j, D̂_l^(j) = 0 is required.

*Proof.* In the DFT domain the outputs agree if and only if
(R′_l − R*_l) α_l^(j) = D̂_l^(j) for all l and j. This system in the single unknown R′_l is
solvable if and only if the stated span condition holds. In the rank-one case it is solved by
R′_l = R*_l + D̂_l^(j) / α_l^(j) for any j with α_l^(j) ≠ 0. Conjugate symmetry of R′ follows
from that of D̂ and α. ∎

*Check:* with one input (E1 or white noise), the information on every Re μ, profiled over a
free R, is 1e-29 to 1e-34 of the tied layer's, i.e. zero.

### Proposition 5 (information with a free steady branch)

With per-sample weights w_j (the E1 loss uses 1/‖y_j‖²), the Fisher information on θ in model
U, profiled over R on all lines, is

  F_U = Re (1/n) Σ_l Σ_j w_j (D̂⊥_{l,j})ᴴ D̂⊥_{l,j},

where D̂_{l,j} ∈ ℂᵖ is the DFT of ∂(T_θ x^(j))/∂θ at line l. D̂⊥ removes, line by line, the
w-weighted projection onto (α_l^(j))_j:

  D̂⊥_{l,j} = D̂_{l,j} − α_l^(j) (Σ_i w_i ᾱ_l^(i) D̂_{l,i}) / (Σ_i w_i |α_l^(i)|²).

So the poles are locally identifiable in U if and only if the transient derivatives are not
proportional, line by line, to the input spectra across the training set.

*Proof.* Profiling a least-squares problem over parameters that enter linearly replaces the
Jacobian by its projection onto the orthogonal complement of their columns. The R_l columns are
(α_l^(j))_j on line l and do not mix lines. Use Parseval. ∎

*Check* (information on Re μ, free R / tied layer, other parameters profiled):

| inputs | p4: pair, −1, −0.2 | p6: −0.5+1.5i, −0.3+4i, −1, −2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 input (E1 or white noise) | 0 (≤ 1e-31) | 0 (≤ 1e-29) |
| 2 inputs | 0.03–0.09 | 0.02–0.12 |
| 5 circular shifts of 1 input | 0.09–0.23 | 0.13–0.28 |
| E1 training set (200 inputs) | 0.52, 0.48, 0.72 | 0.31, 0.28, 0.34, **0.23** |

*Remarks.*

1. **H1 holds exactly in the rank-one case and fails otherwise.** The plan's formulation ("for
   any ε > 0 …") is too strong. With two or more generic inputs the exact non-identifiable set
   is cut down, and the poles are locally identifiable.
2. **Diversity matters more than richness.** A single input with all lines excited gives zero
   information in U, while two inputs give some. Circular shifts of one input already help,
   because the transient branch is not shift-covariant: it is anchored at t = 0.
3. **The cost on E1 data.** A free steady branch keeps 23–72 % of the information. The
   interval on p6's −2 widens by a factor √(1/0.23) ≈ 2.1.
4. **The weak form of H1.** In ALNO and LFNO the steady branch is also truncated to the lowest
   modes, so the truth is not exactly representable. That truncation floor (1.7e-3 on p4,
   9.8e-4 on p6) lies above the loss change of a shifted slow decay. Together with the reduced
   information, this is the practical non-identifiability seen in E3: on p6, −2 is recovered in
   100 % of runs with the tied layer and in 15 % with a steady branch, and runs at the floor
   still miss real poles.

## 3b. H3: the partial tie

**Model P** (`e2_train.PartialTie`, kind `alno_pt`):

  y = A_θ x + C_R x,  R_l free on a set of lines L (the lowest rfft modes), |R_l| ≤ δ.

δ = 0 is the tied layer. As δ → ∞ on all lines, P approaches model U of Section 3.

**Loss.** ℒ(θ, R) = Σ_j w_j ‖A_θ x^(j) + C_R x^(j) − y^(j)‖² (the E1 loss: w_j = 1/‖y^(j)‖²).

**Tied estimate.** θ̂₀ is a local minimiser of ℒ(·, 0) with positive definite Hessian H.

**Cross Hessian.** G = ∂²ℒ/∂θ ∂R at (θ̂₀, 0), with R counted as (Re R_l, Im R_l). R enters
linearly and does not interact with θ in the model, so G = 2 Σ_j w_j J_θ^(j)ᵀ J_R^(j) exactly.

### Theorem 6 (H3: the partial tie moves the poles by at most C δ)

Under these assumptions there are δ₀ > 0 and a neighbourhood U of θ̂₀ such that, for δ < δ₀,
every constrained local minimiser (θ̂_δ, R̂_δ) with θ̂_δ ∈ U satisfies

  θ̂_δ − θ̂₀ = −H⁻¹ G R̂_δ + O(δ²),

and hence, for each Re μ_k,

  |Re μ̂_k(δ) − Re μ̂_k(0)| ≤ C_k δ + O(δ²),  C_k = Σ_{l∈L} |(H⁻¹G)_{k,l}|,

where |·| is the modulus of the pair of entries for (Re R_l, Im R_l).

*Proof.*

- **Implicit function.** ∇_θ ℒ(θ̂₀, 0) = 0 and ∇²_θ ℒ = H > 0. By the implicit-function
  theorem there is a smooth map R ↦ θ(R) near R = 0, unique in some U, with
  ∇_θ ℒ(θ(R), R) = 0 and Dθ(0) = −H⁻¹G.
- **The constrained minimiser lies on that map.** θ is not constrained, so a constrained local
  minimiser with θ̂_δ ∈ U has ∇_θ ℒ(θ̂_δ, R̂_δ) = 0. Hence θ̂_δ = θ(R̂_δ).
- **Bound.** Taylor's theorem and ‖R̂_δ‖_∞ ≤ δ give the bound. For a complex R_l with
  |R_l| ≤ 1, the supremum of a Re R_l + b Im R_l is √(a² + b²). ∎

*Corollaries.*

1. **With exact data the poles do not move.** If y = A_θ* x exactly and θ* is a strict
   minimiser of the free-R loss (Proposition 5 gives this locally for diverse inputs), then
   (θ*, 0) attains ℒ = 0 for every δ, so θ̂_δ = θ*. The plan's "pole error grows in
   proportion to δ" refers to the deviation caused by a model mismatch or noise: the
   residual is what R_res absorbs, and G couples it to the poles. The pole error is
   bounded by |θ̂₀ − θ*| + C δ.
2. **The deviation saturates.** Let δ_sat = ‖R̂_∞‖_∞ be the size of the free-R (on L)
   solution. For δ ≥ δ_sat the bound on R_res is inactive and θ̂_δ = θ̂_∞. The deviation is
   therefore at most min(C δ, |θ̂_∞ − θ̂₀|).
3. **A truth-free design rule.** H and G are evaluated at θ̂₀, which is computed from the data
   alone. Choosing δ ≤ ε / C_k guarantees, to first order, that pole k moves by at most ε from
   the tied estimate. Large C_k marks weakly identified poles: C_k ∝ 1/λ(H) along that pole.

*Check* (`h3_check.py`).

- **Setup.**
  - The model-P estimator is computed directly, by L-BFGS with continuation in δ and a second
    start from the tied fit, on the E2 training data (200 inputs, 64 modes).
  - The data come from the first-order hold, so θ̂₀ is biased by the band-limited floor
    (Lemma 1, Remark 1).
- **Results.**
  - The fit error decreases monotonically in δ.
  - The first-order prediction −H⁻¹G R̂_δ matches the observed deviation to two digits for
    every δ, including deviations of 16 %.
  - The bound C δ holds everywhere and is 10–50 × loose, because it assumes the worst R.

| p4 multisine | −1 | 11.8 | 3.2e-5 | 2.1e-4 | 1.8e-3 | |

*Remarks.*

1. **This is the H3 trade-off.** On p6 E1 data, the partial tie lowers the fit error by 21 %
   and multiplies the error of −2 by 13. The size of C_k orders the poles exactly as their
   identifiability does: −2, then −1, then the complex pairs. On p4 multisine, even the
   saturated (free on 64 modes) estimate keeps every pole within 0.2 %. Diverse data keep
   the poles identifiable with a free steady band (Proposition 5).
2. **Relation to E3.**
   - E3 trained with δ = 0.3, beyond saturation here, so its estimator is θ̂_∞.
   - On p4 this estimator is accurate, and training recovered the real poles in 85–95 % of
     runs.
   - On p6 the E3 runs stopped 1.2–3 × above the floor. That is an optimisation failure, not
     a property of the estimator: E3 (p6 fine) used other data, and the minimiser near the
     truth exists.

## 5. Open

- **H3 beyond first order.** Theorem 6 is local (δ < δ₀) and its bound is worst-case. A
  sharper, average-case bound (R̂_δ is not adversarial) and a global statement are open.
- **Global identifiability for full-support inputs.** Theorem 2 needs sparse support. A
  generic global version (an algebraic-geometry argument over x) is not done.
- **Multi-channel and multi-layer.** Out of scope for the theory, as the plan foresees.
  Treated empirically (E3 deep, E5).
- **Noise.** F above is the inverse of the Cramér–Rao bound used in E4, and Proposition 5 gives
  the CRB of model U. A noisy version of Theorem 4's diversity condition, i.e. how many
  diverse inputs restore a given fraction of the tied information, is open.
